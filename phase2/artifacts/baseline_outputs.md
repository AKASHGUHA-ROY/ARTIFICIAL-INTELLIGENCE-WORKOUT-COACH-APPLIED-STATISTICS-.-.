## Test Case 3
**User input:** Beginner user, leg day, knee pain reported.

**Baseline output:**
Yes — here’s a beginner, knee-friendlier leg workout built for a busy college schedule. It focuses on strengthening the muscles that support the knee, uses low-impact movements, and keeps volume manageable. In general, strengthening the quads, hamstrings, glutes, and calves can reduce stress on the knee, and low-impact exercise is usually easier on irritated joints.

A good rule: during the workout, aim for mild discomfort at most, not sharp pain. If a movement makes you limp, causes locking, instability, major swelling, or pain that clearly worsens as you continue, stop and get medical advice. NHS and Mayo Clinic sources also note that low-impact options are preferred when joints are painful.

30-minute knee-friendly leg day

Do this 2 times per week, with at least 1 day between sessions.

Warm-up — 5 minutes

Use one:

easy stationary bike
brisk walk on flat ground
elliptical at easy pace

Start gently. Warm-up and low-impact cardio can reduce joint stress and help you move more comfortably.

Activation — 5 minutes

Do 1–2 rounds:

Quad set — 10 reps, hold each 5 seconds
Sit or lie down with leg straight. Tighten the front of your thigh and press the knee gently down.
Glute bridge — 10 reps
Lift hips slowly, squeeze glutes, lower with control.
Straight-leg raise — 8 reps per side
Keep the working leg straight and move slowly.

These types of exercises are commonly used in knee rehab and early strengthening because they build support with relatively low knee stress.

Main workout — 15 to 18 minutes

Do 2–3 sets of each. Rest about 60–90 seconds between sets.

1) Box squat to chair — 8 to 10 reps
Sit back to a chair or bed, lightly tap, stand back up.
Keep:

feet about shoulder width
weight balanced through midfoot
knees tracking over toes
range of motion shallow at first

2) Romanian deadlift with dumbbells or backpack — 8 to 10 reps
Soft knees, hinge at hips, flat back, stand tall.
This is usually more hip-dominant and often feels better than deep knee-bend movements.

3) Low step-up — 6 to 8 reps each leg
Use a low step. Lean slightly forward, press through whole foot, step down slowly.
If this hurts, swap for another set of bridges.

4) Glute bridge or hip thrust — 10 to 12 reps
Pause for 1 second at the top.

5) Standing calf raise — 12 to 15 reps
Use wall support if needed.

Cooldown — 3 to 5 minutes
gentle hamstring stretch
gentle quad stretch
calf stretch

AAOS notes that stretching after strengthening can help reduce soreness and keep muscles flexible.

What to avoid for now

Until the knee feels calmer, reduce or skip:

deep squats
lunges with a long range of motion
jumping
running sprints
leg extensions with heavy weight
“push through sharp pain” training

If you want leg work without aggravation, it is usually smarter to build tolerance gradually with controlled, low-impact strength work first.

Simple 4-week progression

Week 1: 2 sets of everything
Week 2: 3 sets on the exercises that feel okay
Week 3: add a little weight to RDLs, squats, or bridges
Week 4: add 1–2 reps per set before adding more weight again

Use this pain guide:

0–2/10 pain: okay
3–4/10: okay if it settles soon after
5+/10 or lingering worse pain the next day: back off range, reps, or load
Busy-student version: 15-minute fallback workout

When you’re slammed with classes:

2 minutes easy bike or walk
box squat to chair — 2 x 8
Romanian deadlift — 2 x 8
glute bridge — 2 x 10
calf raise — 2 x 12

That is enough to stay consistent.

When to get checked

See a clinician or physical therapist sooner if:

the knee swells a lot
it locks or gives out
you cannot bear weight normally
pain is getting worse instead of better
this has been going on for more than a couple of weeks

Want me to turn this into a Mon/Wed/Fri beginner lifting schedule with upper body and rest days too?
